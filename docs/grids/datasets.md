# Power grid datasets

This is the list of power-grid frequency logs the hum is matched against. Each entry
says what it covers and why it matters, in plain language.

**Status legend:** ✅ wired into the tool · ✋ files downloaded by hand, then imported · 🔜 planned · 🔑 needs an account, key or request

---

## Power grids (the hum)

**How it works.** Anything plugged into the wall (and many recorders near wiring)
picks up a faint hum at the mains frequency, 50 Hz or 60 Hz. That frequency is never
exactly 50.000. It drifts slightly (49.98, 50.01, 49.99…) as demand rises and falls.
All generators on one connected grid spin in lockstep, so every place on that grid
sees the same drift at the same moment, and each grid's drift is different from every
other grid's. If you pull that drift out of a recording and line it up against logged
grid data, you learn which grid it was and the exact time.

**Limit.** This gives you the grid, not the city. Continental Europe is a single grid
from Portugal to Turkey.

The full method, validation, coverage and lessons are in the README section "Grid frequency from seismometer hum". **Data quality issues we found and repair automatically** (details in
[`architecture.md`](architecture.md#data-repairs-found-by-cross-checking)):

- **France (OSF):** days 1–12 of every month have day and month swapped (2 January is
  stored as 1 February). That's 95,040 rows per month; we swap them back.
- **France (OSF):** timestamps are 17 s early relative to Germany on the same grid,
  stable in every month (r ≈ 0.995 after alignment). This is measured by
  cross-correlation and corrected before France fills Germany's gaps.
- **Great Britain (OSF):** stored in mHz, but with a median near 50, which fools a
  naive unit guess. Verified against NESO: identical to the second (r = 1.0000).
- **Finland (Fingrid and OSF):** timestamps are Finnish wall-clock time (UTC+2/+3), not
  UTC. Fingrid documents this, and its file for the autumn-change day holds 25 hours.
  The OSF Finland files are identical to Fingrid's row for row (r = 1.0000 at zero
  shift). The repeated autumn hour is told apart by order: a row is in the second pass
  when a later wall time has already been seen. Rows are converted to UTC, and the 2–3 h
  that fall into the neighbouring UTC month are written there.
- **Germany and France (OSF):** German wall-clock time, not UTC (CE 2011–2020 was
  1–2 h early). OSF Germany is the Netztransparenz series, which runs 2 s late; both
  are converted from Europe/Berlin and shifted −2 s.
- **NESO:** some years are published as zips with irregular names. The 2014 files use
  day-first dates in UK local time, 2015–2019 carry a UTC offset per row, and from 2020
  the times are plain, which is UTC except in the 2025-04..2025-10 files: those are UTC+1
  (BST) throughout, even after the 2025-10-26 change. The 2025-03 file's last hour is
  the 2025-04 file's first (r = 1.000), the hour 2025-10-31 23:00 UTC is in neither
  file, and London's PMU (UTC) puts NESO an hour late exactly from April to October.
  No other year's March/April seam repeats an hour (2020–2024 and 2026 checked). Every
  fetch checks this seam: a file whose first hour (in UTC) repeats the stored month
  before's last hour is refused with an error rather than stored an hour off, and past
  2026-10 a month is fetched only once the month before is stored (NESO fetches one
  month at a time, in order).

**Test datasets:**
- **IEEE Signal Processing Cup 2016**: recordings from about 9 grids, where the task
  was "which grid is this from?". That is exactly our step 1, so it works as a
  benchmark.

**Getting all of it.** `geoloc refs refresh` (and the dashboard's Refresh buttons) fetch
every month any source holds that is not stored yet, back to the start of each
archive. Months a source answered "no data" for are remembered and asked again after
90 days (recent months of live sources every time).

**Normalization.** Every reference is stored as *deviation from nominal in Hz, on a
1-second UTC grid, rounded to 1 mHz* (see [`compression.md`](compression.md)), so 50 Hz
and 60 Hz grids look alike to the matcher. Coarser
sources (France at 10 s) are interpolated, and finer ones (Finland at 10 Hz) are
averaged down. Each source records its timestamp convention, because not all of them
are UTC.
